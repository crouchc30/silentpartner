How to use Silent Partner

A walk through every page of the site, in plain English. Nothing here is technical, and nothing you do while reading changes your books.

How to move through this
Press the big Next button at the bottom of the screen, or use the → arrow key on your keyboard. You can stop whenever you like and come back later — it remembers the page you were on. To jump straight to something, press All the steps at the top.

There are 0 short pages. Reading all of them takes about fifteen minutes.

Before you start Page 2 of 31

What this site is for

It keeps track of the shop’s money all year, so that “how much can we spend, or give away?” takes a minute instead of a week.

It watches the bank balance, the bills that are coming, and what the shop usually takes in. From those it works out one number: what is genuinely safe to spend today.

In plain words
Think of it as a careful assistant who has read every bill, never forgets one, and will always show you their working.

It is not a bookkeeping program. It will not do your taxes, run payroll, or send invoices. You still need whatever you already use for those.

Before you start Page 3 of 31

Three things it will never do

This matters more than any feature, so it comes first.

  • It never moves money. It cannot reach your bank. It only reads what you type in.
  • It never sends anything to anybody. The weekly update is text you copy and send yourself, if you want to. The only emails it ever sends are codes, to you, about your own account.
  • It never decides. It recommends, and it pushes back when something looks risky. Every decision stays yours.
Worth knowing
Every figure it shows can be traced back to something you typed. If a number ever looks wrong, there is a line of working you can read, and it is on the home page.

Before you start Page 4 of 31

Your first twenty minutes

Do these five things, in this order. After the second one, the big number on the home page starts being real.

  1. Make your account. Covered on the next few pages.
  2. Type in your bank balance. This is the foundation; nothing works properly without it.
  3. Add the bills that come every month. Payroll, taxes, anything regular.
  4. Add the last few weeks of sales. Eight weeks is plenty.
  5. From then on, log expenses as they happen. A minute each.
Worth knowing
You do not have to do it all at once. The site is useful after step 2, and gets more accurate with each one after that.

Opening it Page 5 of 31

Getting in the first time

Two steps, once. After that it is just your email and your password, on any device you like.

  1. Type the word that came with your link and press Continue. Everyone who was sent the link uses the same word.
  2. Press Create an account.
  3. Put in your own email and choose a password. Type the password twice.
  4. Press Create account.
  5. We email you a code. Type it into the box on the next screen and press Confirm.
In plain words
A code, not a link. Links get broken by the thing that opens them — a work inbox that strips them, a phone that opens the wrong browser. A row of digits you read and type works anywhere, including from a phone screen while you sit at the shop computer.
In plain words
The word from the link is just the front door — it is the same for everybody and it is not what protects your figures. Your account is what protects them. Nobody can see a single number without one.
Important
Use an email you can actually open. If you ever forget your password, that is where the code goes.

Opening it Page 6 of 31

The code from the email

The same screen does two jobs: proving a new email address is yours, and proving it is still yours if the password has gone.

  • It can take a minute to arrive. If it has not, look in the junk folder.
  • Type it in, or paste it — spaces do not matter, they get tidied up.
  • A code stops working after an hour.
  • Send me another code gets a fresh one. The moment you do, the old one stops working, so use the newest email.
Worth knowing
If it says the code is not right, the usual cause is reading an older email. Codes arrive in order and only the last one counts.

Opening it Page 7 of 31

Signing in after that

Your email and your password. Nothing else to remember, and it works on any computer, tablet or phone.

  1. Open the link.
  2. Type the word from the link, if it asks.
  3. Put in your email and password, and press Sign in.
In plain words
The books live online now, not on one computer. Sign in on the shop computer, on the laptop at home, on a phone — it is the same set of books, with the same figures, every time.

Opening it Page 8 of 31

Changing your password when you know it

Settings, at any time. It changes your own account only — nobody else’s sign-in is affected and the books are untouched.

Important
Choose a phrase, not a word. Four ordinary words you will remember beat one clever word you will not.

Opening it Page 9 of 31

If you forget your password

This used to be the end of the world. It is now a two-minute inconvenience.

  1. On the sign-in screen, press Forgot your password?
  2. Put in your email and press Email me a code.
  3. Type the code from the email and press Confirm.
  4. Pick a new password, twice, and press Save new password.

You are taken straight into the books afterwards. There is nothing else to do.

In plain words
Nothing in the books is touched by this. You are changing the key to your own account, not the books themselves, and nobody else’s sign-in changes.

Opening it Page 10 of 31

Two of you, at the same time

More than one person can have the site open at once, and nobody’s typing gets lost.

  • Everyone makes their own account, with their own email and their own password.
  • Everyone sees the same books, kept up to date as it happens.
  • If somebody else changes something while you are looking, the page quietly updates and tells you who did it.
  • If you both save at the same moment, both entries are kept. Neither one wipes the other.
Worth knowing
Settings shows you who else has an account on these books, so you can always check.

Opening it Page 11 of 31

Signing out

Press Sign out, on the right of the bar along the top, whenever you walk away from a shared computer.

  • Nothing is lost. Every entry is saved the moment you type it.
  • If you forget, the site signs you out on its own after an hour of being left alone.
  • To get back in, your email and your password.

The home page Page 12 of 31

The big number

This is the whole point of the site. It is the money left over after every bill it knows about, and after setting your cushion aside.

It is deliberately cautious. It has already taken off the bills you have not paid yet, the regular bills coming due, and the cushion you said you always want to keep.

Worth knowing
If it says $0, that is not a fault. It means the bills it knows about use up everything above your cushion. The working on the next page will show you exactly which ones.

Sales we expectYour recent weekly average, multiplied by how cautious you asked it to be.
Bills you haven’t paid yetEvery unpaid expense, listed by name.
Repeat bills coming dueThe regular ones falling inside your window.
CushionThe amount you said you always keep aside. Never counted as spendable.
In plain words
If a number ever surprises you, read this list. The answer is always in one of these lines.

The home page Page 14 of 31

Needs your OK

The site flags anything that looks like it needs a second glance. It never changes or deletes anything by itself — it only asks.

There are three reasons something gets flagged:

Something is missingNo amount, no date, or not marked paid or unpaid. Until it is complete, it is left out of the big number.
Possible duplicateThe same supplier, the same amount, within a few days. Usually it is a real second order — but sometimes it is the same bill typed twice.
Big amountAnything over the figure you set in Settings. Just a “was this right?”
Worth knowing
Press That’s right and it stops asking. Press the expense itself to correct it.

The home page Page 15 of 31

Coming up

The bills heading towards you, soonest first, so nothing arrives as a surprise.

  • Anything overdue is marked in red.
  • Anything due in the next week is marked in amber.
  • This is the same list the big number has already set money aside for.

Putting your numbers in Page 16 of 31

Start here: money in the bank

This is the foundation. Until the site knows your bank balance, every other number is a guess.

  1. Press Bills & sales along the top.
  2. Look up the current balance on your bank’s website or your last statement.
  3. Put the date that balance is from, and the amount.
  4. Press Save balance.
Worth knowing
Do this about once a week. The site counts everything you have logged as paid since that date, so it stays accurate in between — but a fresh balance is always better than a clever guess.

Putting your numbers in Page 17 of 31

Weekly sales

So the site knows roughly what the shop takes in, and can allow for money still to come.

  1. Put in the week-ending date and what the shop took, after refunds.
  2. Press Save sales.
  3. Or, if your till can produce a spreadsheet, press Import sales and choose the file.
In plain words
It only ever counts a portion of expected sales — 60% unless you change it — because a good autumn is not a promise about December. You can change that under Settings.

Putting your numbers in Page 18 of 31

Bills that repeat

Enter payroll, taxes and anything else regular once, and the site will keep setting money aside for them for ever.

  1. Give the bill a name you will recognise.
  2. Put in the amount and the next date it is due.
  3. Choose how often: monthly, quarterly or yearly.
  4. Press Add bill.
Important
This is the single most valuable thing you can set up. It is what stops the site telling you money is safe to spend when payroll is ten days away.

The list of everything

Every expense, newest first, with anything needing attention marked.

  • Use the dropdown to show only unpaid bills, or only things needing your OK.
  • Press Mark paid when a bill is settled — the big number updates straight away.
  • Press Edit to correct anything. Press Delete to remove it.
In plain words
Editing an expense makes the site look at it afresh, so it may ask you to confirm it again. That is deliberate.

Can we afford it?

For when somebody asks for something specific and you need an answer you can defend.

  1. Press Can we afford it? along the top.
  2. Type the amount, and a few words about what it is for.
  3. Press Check it.

The questions it answers Page 22 of 31

...and the answer you get

Not just yes or no. It names the bills at risk and offers a middle option.

If it says hold off, it will tell you how far into your cushion the spending would dig, which bills are at risk, and a smaller figure that would be safe — and when to look again.

Worth knowing
If you need to show somebody why the answer is no, this is the part to show them. Every line of it came from something you typed in.

The questions it answers Page 23 of 31

The Year-end payout

The big one. The owners owns the shop, so the year-end payment is its share of the year — not a gift.

Two things come off before the Club’s share can be worked out:

  1. What the shop needs to open next season without borrowing. You type that figure in.
  2. The manager payouts, as a percentage of what is left.

Whatever survives both is the owners’s check, and the working is shown so you can take it to a meeting.

In plain words
This adds no new arithmetic. It starts from the same safe-to-spend figure as the home page, which has already covered every bill and held your cushion back.

The questions it answers Page 24 of 31

Manager payouts

Splitting the management share between the people who get one.

  • Leave the shares blank and the money is divided evenly.
  • Or put in numbers to weight it. 60 and 40 divides it the same way 6 and 4 does — they do not have to add up to 100.

The questions it answers Page 25 of 31

Are you charging enough?

Works out what something really costs to sell, including your own time, and whether the price covers it.

The important box is minutes of your time. Most small shops look profitable only because nobody is counting the owner’s hours.

Important
If an item only makes money when your time is free, it says so, in those words, and tells you what you lose on each one.

The weekly update

A short, plain summary of where the shop stands, written for you to send to somebody else.

  1. Press Weekly update along the top.
  2. Read it. Change any of the wording you like — it is just text.
  3. Press Copy, then paste it into an email or a text message.
Important
Nothing is ever sent for you. It goes no further than your clipboard until you send it yourself.

Looking after it Page 28 of 31

History: who did what

Every sign-in and every change, with a name and a time against it. Anyone who can open the books can read it.

Press History along the top. The newest thing is at the top. It will tell you, for instance, that Shelly marked an invoice paid on Tuesday afternoon, or that you added a bank balance on Friday.

  • Use the dropdown to show just one person.
  • Tick changes only to hide the sign-ins and see only what was actually altered.
  • Download as a spreadsheet if you want it on paper or for your accountant.
In plain words
Nothing in History can be edited or deleted, by anybody, including whoever set the site up. A record that could be tidied up afterwards would not be worth keeping.
Worth knowing
If a name you do not recognise appears, check Settings — it lists everyone who has an account on these books.

Looking after it Page 29 of 31

Keeping a copy

The books live online and are saved as you type. A backup is a copy of your own, in a file, in case you ever want one.

  • Download a backup saves everything to one file on this computer.
  • Restore backup puts one back. It replaces what is there now, for everybody, so it asks first.
  • Download expenses gives you a plain spreadsheet for your accountant.
Be careful here
Both of those files are plain and readable. Anyone who gets hold of one can read every figure in it. Keep them where you would keep a bank statement, and think twice before emailing one.
In plain words
You do not need to take a backup to avoid losing work. Your work is saved online the moment you type it, and it is still there if this computer is dropped in a puddle. A backup is for having a copy of your own.

If something looks wrong

The usual causes, and what to do about them.

The big number looks too lowRead the working on the home page. Usually a large unpaid bill, or a repeat bill falling inside the window.
The big number says $0Your known bills use up everything above the cushion. The working will name them.
A number has not changedCheck whether the expense is waiting under Needs your OK. Incomplete entries are left out until they are finished.
My password will not workCheck Caps Lock first. If it still will not, press Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen and we will email you a code to set a new one.
The word from the link is not enoughThat word only opens the front door. After it you need your own email and password. If you have not made an account yet, press Create an account.
The code has not arrivedGive it a minute, then look in the junk folder. Send me another code on that same screen gets a fresh one.
It says my code is wrongCheck you are reading the newest email. Asking for another code stops the one before it from working.
It says my code has expiredCodes last an hour. Press Send me another code.
Somebody changed something I did not expectOpen History. It will tell you who, what, and when.
Does closing the page lose my work?No. Every entry is saved online the moment you type it. Close the tab, change computers, come back next week: sign in and it is all there.
Something was deleted by mistakeCheck History to see what went and who removed it, then type it back in. If a lot went at once, restore a backup under Settings.
The page looks brokenClose the tab and open the link again. Nothing is lost; everything is saved as you type.
